## Why fewer samples are worth the engineering

A path tracer renders each frame by estimating light transport from Monte Carlo
samples. At a low sample count the estimator's variance shows up as stochastic
noise. For independent samples the variance falls inversely with sample count,

$$
\mathrm{Var}[\hat{L}_N] = \frac{\sigma^2}{N},
\qquad
\sigma_{\hat{L}_N} = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{N}},
$$

so halving the noise costs roughly four times the samples. That cost is paid on
every frame of every shot. Denoising a cheaper render is the only way out that
does not scale the farm bill with the square of the quality target.

## How it works

The system combines motion-compensated temporal accumulation with AOV-guided
spatial filtering, applied across the beauty and lighting passes.

Temporal processing came first in the design. Adjacent frames are aligned to the
current frame, validation thresholds reject unreliable history, particularly
around disocclusions and changing visibility, and the valid remainder is
accumulated. That reduces noise and frame-to-frame flicker together, rather than
trading one against the other.

Spatially, Non-Local Means computes weights from image-patch similarity combined
with differences in albedo, normal, depth and world position. Those guides stop
samples being gathered across geometry and material boundaries. Outlier handling
suppresses bright fireflies in high-variance specular and transmission
components without applying the same aggressive smoothing to the rest of the
image.

Each guide carries an independent sigma parameter controlling its contribution
to the filter weights:

| AOV / pass | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| `rgba / beauty` | Noisy combined render |
| `diffuse / indirect diffuse` | Diffuse lighting components |
| `specular / indirect specular` | Direct and indirect reflected lighting |
| `refraction` | Transmission component |
| `albedo` | Material colour independent of illumination |
| `pos_world` | Surface correspondence and separation in world space |
| `depth.Z` | Rejection across depth discontinuities |
| `normal_world` | Surface orientation discontinuities |

The method is deterministic. There is no training data, no network and no
learned weights. Filtering is driven by renderer outputs, motion estimation and
artist-defined parameters, which is what made it predictable enough to sit
inside a nightly render pipeline.

## Shipping it

The denoiser was delivered three ways, because three different people needed it:
a standalone Linux and Windows application for batch work, a Foundry Nuke
plug-in for compositing artists, and a C++/Python API for integration into the
Maya–RenderMan pipeline. In production it ran as a post-render processing stage
managed by Pixar Tractor.

It was deployed on *The Fixies*, *The Fixies: Top Secret*, *Fixies vs. Crabots*
and *Fairy-teens*. On selected production shots it reduced visible Monte Carlo
noise by up to **7×**, while preserving fur, hair, specular highlights and
bump-mapped surface detail. Across production it helped cut estimated rendering
costs by about **30%**.

The [result reel](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dqq-chYzEYs) shows the temporal
behaviour on real shots.
